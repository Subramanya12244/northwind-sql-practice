# Q17 · Employees Above Average Revenue (Correlated Subquery)

> **Revision Card**
> **Category:** Correlated Subqueries | **Difficulty:** Medium
> **Concepts:** INNER JOIN (multi-table) · GROUP BY · HAVING · Subquery (non-correlated) · Aggregates (SUM, AVG)
> **Outcome:** ✅ correct first attempt
> **Watch out for:** group by `employee_id` (not name), and keep the duplicated revenue formula in sync across outer and inner queries.

## 1. Problem

Same business need as Q7 — flag above-average-revenue employees — solved via `HAVING` and a nested subquery rather than a CTE.

**Objective:** Return employees whose total revenue exceeds the average revenue across all employees, using a `HAVING` clause referencing a subquery.

**Tables:** `employees`, `orders`, `order_details`

**Expected output:**

| Column Name | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| employee_id | Unique identifier of the employee |
| first_name | Employee's first name |
| last_name | Employee's last name |
| total_revenue | Sum of discounted revenue generated by this employee |

| employee_id | first_name | last_name | total_revenue |
|-------------|------------|-----------|---------------|
| 4 | Margaret | Peacock | 232890.85 |
| 3 | Janet | Leverling | 202812.84 |
| 1 | Nancy | Davolio | 192107.60 |

*(Sample values are illustrative, based on the standard Northwind dataset, and intended to show shape/format — not guaranteed to match your exact data instance.)*

## 2. My Approach

1. Join `employees`, `orders`, and `order_details`; aggregate discounted revenue per employee.
2. In `HAVING`, compare that total against a subquery that recomputes revenue per employee and averages it.
3. Sort by total revenue descending.

## 3. My Solution (as submitted)

```sql
SELECT
    e.employee_id,
    e.first_name,
    e.last_name,
    SUM(od.unit_price * od.quantity * (1 - od.discount)) AS total_revenue
FROM employees e
JOIN orders o ON o.employee_id = e.employee_id
JOIN order_details od ON od.order_id = o.order_id
GROUP BY e.employee_id, e.first_name, e.last_name
HAVING SUM(od.unit_price * od.quantity * (1 - od.discount)) > (
    SELECT AVG(rev)
    FROM (
        SELECT SUM(od2.unit_price * od2.quantity * (1 - od2.discount)) AS rev
        FROM orders o2
        JOIN order_details od2 ON od2.order_id = o2.order_id
        GROUP BY o2.employee_id
    ) sub
)
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
```

## 6. Why This Works

**Structurally identical to Q16, applied to employees instead of customers:** the same duplication trade-off applies — the revenue formula appears twice, once for the outer aggregate, once inside the inner subquery that computes the global average across all employees.

The inner subquery is non-correlated: it recomputes per-employee revenue over *all* employees and averages it, producing a single company-wide figure that is independent of whichever employee the outer query is currently evaluating. `HAVING` (not `WHERE`) is required because the filter compares an aggregate (`SUM(...)`) against that scalar.

## 7. Common Pitfalls (generic)

- Same risk as Q16: letting the revenue logic drift between the two copies if the formula needs to change.
- Grouping by name instead of `employee_id` (two employees could share a name, collapsing their rows incorrectly).
